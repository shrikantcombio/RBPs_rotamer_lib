# Backbone deviation and side-chain conformation

Does a backbone change between the unbound and the RNA-bound protein go with a side-chain change,
and is that coupling specific to the RNA-binding interface?

Generated by `script/backbone_deviation.py`; tables are in `output_files/backbone_deviation/`.

```bash
python script/backbone_deviation.py            # ~1 min; --rebuild-covariates re-reads B-factors
```

## Data

- `input_files/ALL_AA_BACKBONE_OMEGA_SIDECHAIN_TORSION_ANGLES_187_clean.tsv`: 187 PRDBv3 complexes,
  bound and unbound residues paired.
- Every bound complex is an X-ray structure. The unbound protein is an X-ray structure, except for
  14 complexes with no free crystal structure, where an NMR structure was used.
- Resolution: `PRDBv3_dataset/PRDBv3.0/PRDBv3_info.csv`. Side-chain B-factors are read from the
  paired `_C.pdb` / `_U.pdb` files.
- 59,240 residues: interface 5,830, non-interface surface 48,486, core 4,924. Left out: PRO
  (ring pucker, not a staggered rotamer), residues without φ/ψ in either state, and 78 cis/trans
  peptide flips.

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Δφψ | backbone change, √(Δφ² + Δψ²), shortest-arc degrees |
| Rotamer switch | the χ's library bin differs between bound and unbound: sp3 χ uses p / t / m; terminal sp2 χ uses residue-specific bins (`rotamer_definitions.py`) |
| χ analysed | χ1, χ2, χ3 (ARG, LYS, MET) and χ4 (ARG, LYS). Single-bin χ (ASP/PHE/TYR χ2, GLU/GLN χ3) cannot switch |
| ARG χ5 | the guanidinium is planar and NH1/NH2 are equivalent, so χ5 ≈ 0° and ≈ 180° are the same conformation. Only the deviation mod 180° is analysed |
| Side-chain B | mean B-factor of side-chain heavy atoms, z-scored within each structure |
| Region | I = interface, N = non-interface surface, C = core (PRince `.int` / `.sur`) |

## Figures

### `dose_response.png`

- Rows χ1–χ5.
- Left column: rotamer switch rate by Δφψ.
- Right column: mean |Δχ| of residues that keep their rotamer.
- Error bars are 95% intervals from resampling complexes. Cells with fewer than 20 residues are
  not drawn (all cells are in `dose_response.tsv`).

### `adjusted_odds_ratios.png`

Logistic regression of a switch on:
- log2(1 + Δφψ) × region,
- residue type,
- bound secondary structure,
- bound and unbound resolution,
- side-chain B z-score.

Standard errors are clustered by complex. Δφψ is centred on its median, so the region rows
compare regions at the same backbone change.

| Model | Data | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Main | 173 complexes with X-ray unbound | full |
| All complexes | 187 complexes, including NMR unbound | no resolution / B-factor terms |
| Main, without high-B | main | drops residues with side-chain B z > 2 in either state |
| Main + upstream χ switch | χ2–χ4 only | adds whether an earlier χ switched; separates a direct backbone effect from one carried through χ1 / χ2 |

### `backbone_dependent_prediction.png`

Scope: residues whose full rotamer and 30° (φ, ψ) library bin both change.

**Score.** For each such residue:
- S = log P(rot_B | bb_B) − log P(rot_B | bb_U) + log P(rot_U | bb_U) − log P(rot_U | bb_B).
- S > 0 means the residue moved to the rotamer the backbone-dependent library favours on its new
  backbone.

**Probabilities.**
- Leave-one-complex-out counts.
- Bound and unbound states pooled.
- Smoothed towards the backbone-independent distribution.

**Null.** The same library, with each residue's (bound, unbound) backbone pair taken from another
residue of the same type.

## Results

**1. The backbone effect fades along the side chain.** Odds ratio per doubling of Δφψ (main
model):

| | χ1 | χ2 | χ3 | χ4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Surface | 1.61 | 1.28 | 1.21 | 1.13 |
| Interface | 1.62 | 1.26 | 1.08 (0.99–1.18) | 1.07 (0.98–1.17) |
| Interface, adjusted for upstream switch | — | 1.17 | 0.98 | 1.02 |

At χ3 and χ4 the backbone acts through χ1/χ2. An upstream switch has an odds ratio of 2.8–3.1.

**2. The coupling is not specific to binding.** The interface/surface slope ratio is about 1:
- χ1: 1.01 (0.94–1.08)
- χ2: 0.98
- χ3: 0.89
- χ4: 0.95

**3. Binding-specific effect.** At the same backbone change, interface residues switch more:

| | χ1 | χ2 | χ3 | χ4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interface vs surface | 1.47 (1.34–1.61) | 1.26 | 1.41 | 1.39 |
| Same, adjusted for upstream switch | — | 1.19 | 1.29 | 1.22 |

This holds in all sensitivity models.

**4. Many switches need no backbone change.** Share of switches with Δφψ < 20°:

| Region | χ1 | χ2 | χ3–χ4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interface | 51% | 61% | 66–67% |
| Surface | 56% | 66% | 70–72% |

χ3/χ4 switch in 24–39% of residues even at Δφψ < 5°.

**5. When the backbone moves, the side chain follows backbone-dependent preferences.**
- Overall, 60.0% of switches have S > 0, against 49.7% for shuffled backbones (95% range
  48.7–50.7).
- The share rises from 55–56% at Δφψ < 20° to 61–64% at Δφψ ≥ 20°.
- Interface (58%) is not higher than surface (60%).

**6. ARG χ5 stays planar.** The deviation is 1–2° with no trend against Δφψ.

**Summary.** Backbone change accounts for part of the rearrangement of the proximal side chain,
in the direction expected from backbone-dependent rotamer preferences. It does so to the same
degree inside and outside the interface. Independently of the backbone, interface residues switch
rotamers about 1.2–1.5 times more often at every χ.

## Caveats

- Static bound/unbound pairs show association, not causation. Binding, crystal packing and
  coordinate error can move both backbone and side chain. Side-chain B-factor is the strongest
  covariate (OR about 1.5–1.75 per SD).
- The structure files carry no alternate conformations, so altloc filtering was not possible.
- Core χ3 (about 360 residues) and χ4 (about 150) estimates are imprecise.
